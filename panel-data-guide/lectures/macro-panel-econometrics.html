Cross-Sectional Dependence, Heterogeneity and Long-Run Panel Models

A methodological lecture for country-year and other macro panels. It develops the common-factor structure, dependence and heterogeneity diagnostics, second-generation unit-root testing, panel cointegration, and heterogeneous long-run estimators.

1. Panel structure and the common-factor problem

Let \(i=1,\ldots,N\) index countries or other macro units and \(t=1,\ldots,T\) index time. A heterogeneous panel regression is

\[y_{it}=\alpha_i+\boldsymbol{\beta}_i'\mathbf{x}_{it}+\boldsymbol{\lambda}_i'\mathbf{f}_t+\varepsilon_{it},\]

where \(\alpha_i\) is a unit-specific intercept, \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_i\) is a potentially heterogeneous slope vector, \(\mathbf{f}_t\) is a vector of unobserved common factors, and \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_i\) contains unit-specific factor loadings. Global interest rates, commodity prices, financial conditions, technology and common policy shocks can induce dependence across countries even after observed controls are included.

Core distinction. Cross-sectional dependence concerns dependence across \(i\) at a given time; slope heterogeneity concerns variation in \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_i\). They are different features and require different diagnostics and estimator responses.

For macro panels, asymptotics often require both \(N\) and \(T\) to be sufficiently informative. A large-\(N\), short-\(T\) micro-panel design is not interchangeable with a country panel having moderate or large \(T\).

2. Cross-sectional dependence

After a baseline model is estimated, let \(\widehat{\rho}_{ij}\) be the pairwise correlation between residuals for units \(i\) and \(j\). Pesaran's CD statistic is based on

\[CD=\sqrt{\frac{2T}{N(N-1)}}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}\widehat{\rho}_{ij}.\]

The null is cross-sectional independence. Rejection indicates that conventional first-generation panel procedures relying on cross-sectional independence may not be appropriate without adjustment. The test should be considered alongside the economic structure of common shocks and residual diagnostics.

Implementation: Stata's xtcd2 is distributed with xtdcce2. Run it on the variables or, more importantly, on residuals from a defensible baseline model.

3. Slope heterogeneity

In a homogeneous specification, \(\boldsymbol{\beta}_i=\boldsymbol{\beta}\) for all \(i\). The Pesaran–Yamagata delta test assesses this restriction using dispersion in unit-specific slope estimates relative to a pooled estimate. The economic question is whether a common response is credible across countries with different institutions, production structures, energy mixes and policy regimes.

  • If homogeneity is defensible, pooled estimators may gain precision.
  • If slopes differ, Mean Group or CCE-MG/AMG estimators allow unit-specific slopes.
  • PMG imposes a common long-run coefficient vector but allows short-run coefficients and adjustment speeds to vary.

4. Panel unit roots: first versus second generation

FamilyExamplesKey maintained structure
First generationLLC, IPS, Fisher-ADF, Fisher-PPCross-sectional independence, or an adjustment that makes it credible.
Second generationCADF and CIPSCross-sectional averages proxy unobserved common factors.

The cross-sectionally augmented Dickey–Fuller regression includes lagged levels and cross-sectional averages. The CIPS statistic is the cross-sectional average of individual CADF statistics:

\[CIPS=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}CADF_i.\]

In Stata, xtcips implements the Pesaran CIPS test and requires a balanced panel. Deterministic terms (intercept or trend), lag selection and sample comparability must be specified and reported. A test result should not be treated as an integration-order label independent of those choices.

5. Panel cointegration

Cointegration is considered when variables are appropriately established as I(1) and theory supports a long-run equilibrium relation. Residual-based tests include Pedroni and Kao. Westerlund's error-correction tests ask whether deviations from long-run equilibrium generate adjustment.

\[\Delta y_{it}=\alpha_i+\phi_i\big(y_{i,t-1}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_i'\mathbf{x}_{i,t-1}\big)+\sum_j\psi_{ij}\Delta y_{i,t-j}+\sum_j\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}'\Delta\mathbf{x}_{i,t-j}+\varepsilon_{it}.\]

The null in the Westerlund error-correction framework is no error correction (no cointegration); the alternative can allow adjustment in some or all panel members depending on the statistic. When cross-sectional dependence is material, bootstrap inference or a dependence-aware implementation is important. Cointegration tests do not by themselves establish a common long-run coefficient vector.

6. Estimator families

6.1 Mean Group (MG)

Estimate a separate time-series regression for each unit and average the coefficients:

\[\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{MG}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_i.\]

MG allows slope heterogeneity but does not automatically remove bias from unobserved common factors. It is a useful heterogeneous benchmark when each unit has enough time observations.

6.2 Common Correlated Effects (CCE / CCEMG)

CCE augments unit-specific regressions with cross-sectional averages of the dependent and explanatory variables, and where needed their lags. These averages proxy the unobserved common factors. CCEMG averages the unit-specific CCE slope estimates. The rank condition and the number of cross-sectional averages relative to the factor structure matter.

6.3 Augmented Mean Group (AMG)

AMG estimates a common dynamic process from a pooled first-difference model with time effects, then includes that process in unit-specific regressions. It allows heterogeneous slopes while controlling for an estimated common dynamic component.

6.4 Pooled Mean Group (PMG)

For an error-correction representation, PMG constrains the long-run vector to be common, while allowing short-run coefficients, intercepts, variances and adjustment speeds to differ:

\[\Delta y_{it}=\phi_i\big(y_{i,t-1}-\boldsymbol{\theta}'\mathbf{x}_{i,t-1}\big)+\sum_{j=1}^{p_i-1}\psi_{ij}\Delta y_{i,t-j}+\sum_{j=0}^{q_i-1}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{ij}'\Delta\mathbf{x}_{i,t-j}+\mu_i+\varepsilon_{it}.\]

The long-run restriction \(\boldsymbol{\theta}_i=\boldsymbol{\theta}\) is substantive. Compare PMG with MG and dependence-aware estimators; do not select PMG solely because it produces a smaller standard error.

6.5 CS-ARDL

CS-ARDL augments a dynamic heterogeneous ARDL with current and lagged cross-sectional averages. Unit-specific long-run effects can be formed from the distributed-lag coefficients and then averaged. This combines dynamic adjustment with a proxy for unobserved common factors.

7. Empirical workflow

  1. Describe \(N\), \(T\), balancedness, missingness, frequency and the economic comparability of units.
  2. Estimate transparent baseline FE/RE specifications and inspect residual dependence.
  3. Test cross-sectional dependence and slope homogeneity.
  4. Choose first- or second-generation unit-root procedures accordingly; specify intercepts, trends and lags.
  5. Test cointegration only when the integration orders and theoretical relation warrant it.
  6. Compare MG, CCE-MG, AMG, PMG and CS-ARDL under explicit restrictions.
  7. Report residual dependence, serial correlation, adjustment coefficients, lag sensitivity, sample sensitivity and long-run coefficient comparisons.

8. Stata computational implementation

The downloadable do-file in the guide contains a complete workflow for baseline FE/RE/CRE, Pesaran CD, CIPS, Pedroni/Westerlund cointegration, PMG and CS-ARDL. Map the identifier, time and variable names at the top of the script.

xtset country_id year

* Slope homogeneity
xthst ln_gdp ln_energy ln_co2

* Cross-sectional dependence
xtcd2 ln_gdp

* CIPS (balanced panel)
xtcips ln_gdp, maxlags(2) bglags(1)

* Cointegration after establishing I(1) properties
xtcointtest pedroni ln_gdp ln_energy ln_co2
xtcointtest westerlund ln_gdp ln_energy ln_co2, demean

* PMG: common long-run vector
xtpmg D.ln_gdp D.ln_energy D.ln_co2, ///
    lr(L.ln_gdp ln_energy ln_co2) ec(ec) replace pmg

* CS-ARDL with cross-sectional averages
xtdcce2 D.ln_gdp ln_energy ln_co2 D.(ln_energy ln_co2), ///
    crosssectional(D.ln_gdp D.ln_energy D.ln_co2) ///
    cr_lags(0 3 3) ///
    lr(L.ln_gdp ln_energy ln_co2) lr_options(ardl)
Implementation discipline. The commands above are templates, not a universal sequence. Check panel balance for CIPS, choose deterministic components and lag orders, and confirm that the long-run estimator's restrictions match the research design. 9. Problem set

  1. Write a factor-augmented panel regression and explain how omitted common factors can generate cross-sectional dependence and bias pooled estimates.
  2. Explain why rejection of cross-sectional independence changes the choice between first- and second-generation panel unit-root tests.
  3. State the restriction that distinguishes PMG from MG. Give an economic setting where a common long-run elasticity is plausible and one where it is not.
  4. Explain why a significant Westerlund statistic does not establish that all countries share the same long-run coefficient vector.
  5. Compare CCEMG and AMG in terms of how each proxies unobserved common factors.
