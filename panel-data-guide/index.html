Decision architecture

How the guide reasons

A staged assessment, not a one-test automatic selector.

  1. Identify the panel: entity/time dimensions, balancedness, missingness and variable variation.
  2. Choose the research branch: macroeconomic time-series properties or microeconomic identification/design.
  3. Assess dependence and heterogeneity: first- versus second-generation panel procedures; homogeneous versus heterogeneous estimators.
  4. Assess dynamics: stationarity, cointegration, endogeneity, treatment assignment and outcome type.
  5. Select an estimator family: FE/RE/CRE, DiD, GMM, CCE/AMG/PMG/CS-ARDL or nonlinear panel models.
  6. Validate inference: robust standard errors, residual diagnostics, identification and sensitivity checks.

Pooled OLS, RE, FE, FD and Mundlak

Runs the wagepan and crime4 empirical exercises. Reports the union coefficient and entity-clustered standard errors, performs a joint Mundlak test, and compares deterrence elasticities under pooled OLS and two-way FE.

  • Packages: wooldridge, pandas, statsmodels, linearmodels.
  • Data are loaded from the Wooldridge dataset package.

from linearmodels.panel import PanelOLS, RandomEffects
import wooldridge as wd
panel = wd.data("wagepan").set_index(["nr", "year"])
fe = PanelOLS.from_formula(
    "lwage ~ union + married + expersq + TimeEffects + EntityEffects",
    data=panel
).fit(cov_type="clustered", cluster_entity=True)
re = RandomEffects.from_formula(
    "lwage ~ 1 + union + married + expersq", data=panel
).fit(cov_type="clustered", cluster_entity=True)
print(fe.summary)
print(re.summary)

Methodological reference

Estimator and test guides

Use these summaries to understand the role of each procedure. Exact implementation depends on the data structure, deterministic components, estimator assumptions, and the research design.

Specification

Pooled OLS, FE, RE and CRE

Separate within-unit variation from between-unit variation and model time-invariant unobserved heterogeneity.

  • FE absorbs time-invariant unit effects.
  • RE requires the unit effect to be uncorrelated with included regressors.
  • Mundlak / correlated RE models the correlation through unit means.
  • Use F, LM and Hausman-type comparisons as specification evidence, not mechanical rules.
Dependence

Pesaran CD and common factors

Cross-sectional dependence can invalidate conventional first-generation panel tests and standard errors.

  • Consider Pesaran CD and residual dependence diagnostics.
  • Common correlated effects (CCE) uses cross-sectional averages.
  • Driscoll–Kraay changes inference under broad dependence conditions when T is sufficiently informative.
  • PCSE requires a defensible covariance structure.
Heterogeneity

Pesaran–Yamagata delta

Assess whether slope homogeneity is a defensible restriction in panels with suitable N and T asymptotics.

  • Mean Group (MG) estimates unit-specific relationships.
  • CCEMG combines unit-specific slopes with common-factor controls.
  • AMG accommodates unobserved common dynamic processes.
  • PMG imposes common long-run coefficients while allowing short-run heterogeneity.
Panel stationarity

First- and second-generation unit roots

Choose procedures in light of dependence, deterministic terms and the relative dimensions N and T.

  • First generation: LLC, IPS, Fisher-ADF and Fisher-PP.
  • Second generation: CADF and CIPS (cross-sectionally augmented IPS).
  • Specify intercept/trend and lag selection consistently.
  • Do not infer common integration order from one test alone.
Long-run relationships

Panel cointegration

Cointegration analysis is relevant for nonstationary I(1) variables when a long-run equilibrium relation is theoretically meaningful.

  • Pedroni and Kao residual-based tests.
  • Westerlund error-correction-based tests.
  • Account for cross-sectional dependence and slope heterogeneity.
  • Consider bootstrap critical values where appropriate.
Dynamic panels

Difference and System GMM

For dynamic panels with large N and short T, GMM may address lagged dependent variables and selected endogenous regressors.

  • State the moment conditions and instrument timing.
  • Report AR(1) and AR(2) tests in differenced residuals.
  • Report Hansen/Sargan and difference-in-Hansen tests where relevant.
  • Control instrument proliferation and report instrument counts.
Macroeconomic dynamics

PMG-ARDL, MG and CS-ARDL

Dynamic heterogeneous panels require explicit assumptions about long-run homogeneity, common factors and cross-sectional dependence.

  • PMG constrains long-run coefficients to be common.
  • MG allows long-run and short-run coefficients to vary by unit.
  • CS-ARDL augments dynamic equations with cross-sectional averages and lags.
  • CCE-MG / AMG are alternatives under common-factor dependence.
Causal inference

Difference-in-Differences and event studies

Use a design-based pathway when treatment timing and a comparison group identify a policy effect.

  • Define treatment, comparison group and treatment timing.
  • Assess parallel trends and anticipation.
  • For staggered adoption, use estimators robust to treatment-effect heterogeneity.
  • Cluster uncertainty at the assignment level where justified.
Limited outcomes

Binary and count panel models

Match the conditional mean or likelihood to the outcome support and the unit-effects structure.

  • Conditional FE logit for suitable binary FE settings.
  • RE logit/probit requires distributional assumptions on unit effects.
  • FE Poisson can estimate conditional mean effects for count outcomes.
  • Interpret marginal effects and incidental-parameter limitations carefully.
Scope and limitations. CSV inspection computes structural summaries in the browser; it does not automatically run Pesaran CD, slope-homogeneity, panel unit-root, cointegration, Hausman, GMM, or causal-identification tests. Those results must be obtained using a suitable statistical package or supplied by the researcher. Recommendations are conditional research guidance, not proof of identification or estimator validity.
