01 / JOB MARKET PAPER

Job Market Paper

A structural analysis of fiscal-policy transmission in South Korea.

MACROECONOMICS · FISCAL POLICY · HANK

Which Stimulates More? Spending and Transfers in Korea’s Wage-Rigid Economy

Read Job Market Paper ↗

ABSTRACT

This paper examines how fiscal policy transmits through government spending and direct transfers in South Korea using a Heterogeneous Agent New Keynesian (HANK) model with wage rigidity and debt-sensitive taxation. Calibrated to Korean macroeconomic data, the model captures key institutional features, including household heterogeneity, liquidity constraints, and labor market stickiness. A 1% increase in government spending generates a peak output gain of 0.5% that persists over 60 quarters, while an equivalent transfer shock yields a sharper 0.2% consumption increase that fades within 10 quarters. Tax feedback plays a critical role—spending raises labor income tax rates by up to 0.6 percentage points, compared to only 0.3 points under transfers—affecting household behavior and fiscal sustainability. Sensitivity analysis shows that fiscal multipliers decline sharply with higher steady-state bond supply, stronger tax adjustment rules, and more aggressive monetary tightening. For instance, increasing the bond supply target from 30% to 100% of GDP reduces the impact multiplier by over 46%, while a shift to aggressive monetary policy lowers it by more than 50%. These findings highlight the trade-offs between short-run stabilization and long-run fiscal discipline, offering new insights into optimal policy coordination in an unequal, wage-rigid economy.

02 / ECONOMICS PROFILE

Research profile

Economic questions, policy mechanisms, and empirical evidence.

I am Tuhin G. M. Al Mamun (Tuhin Al Mamun), an economist working on monetary and fiscal policy, environmental and climate economics, and applied macroeconomic analysis. My research examines how policy decisions and economic shocks affect macroeconomic activity, households, public finances, and environmental outcomes.

My work combines structural macroeconomic modeling with applied time-series and panel econometrics. I use DSGE and Heterogeneous-Agent New Keynesian (HANK) frameworks to study policy transmission, household heterogeneity, fiscal sustainability, and macroeconomic adjustment.

I also conduct empirical policy evaluation, including causal analysis of fiscal, monetary, and environmental interventions across countries and over time.

Alongside my research, I develop open teaching resources for empirical economics. Students often learn econometric methods in class but find it hard to use them to write a complete paper. My course sites teach the methods, and my interactive research guides in time series, panel data, causal inference, DSGE modelling, machine learning, Bayesian econometrics and Markov-switching models show how to apply them, step by step, from raw data to a finished empirical paper. The same material is collected in a free handbook, From Data to Decision.

01 / STATISTICS PROFILE

Research profile

Statistical theory, methodology, inference, and computation.

My research interests focus on Bayesian statistics, statistical inference, hierarchical modeling, and computational statistics. I am interested in developing statistical methods for complex data structures involving dependence, group heterogeneity, latent structure, and high-dimensional parameter spaces.

My methodological work includes Bayesian nonparametric modeling, hierarchical Dirichlet processes, regularization, and simulation-based computation. A central objective is to connect practical estimation algorithms with rigorous theoretical guarantees, including posterior consistency and asymptotic properties.

I am also interested in statistical inference for dependent and heterogeneous data, semiparametric methods, mixture models, and computational approaches to model selection and estimation.

Methods and computational tools

Bayesian computation, statistical inference, Monte Carlo methods, simulation, and numerical optimization.
