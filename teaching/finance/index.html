01 / OVERVIEW

About this course

This course develops the theory and quantitative practice of financial decision-making. It covers return measurement, risk and expected utility, portfolio choice, asset pricing, fixed income, derivatives, corporate finance, market efficiency, banking, risk management, behavioral finance, and empirical financial econometrics.

What you will learn

  1. Calculate returns and characterize risk under alternative probability distributions.
  2. Derive mean-variance portfolios and asset-pricing relations.
  3. Apply no-arbitrage methods to bonds, forwards, and options.
  4. Evaluate capital budgeting, capital structure, and payout decisions.
  5. Analyze financial intermediation and systemic risk.
  6. Estimate and evaluate empirical financial models with reproducible workflows.

How the course is organized

Each lecture session has a full set of written notes: learning objectives, formal development with derivations, worked examples, a problem set, a computational lab and readings. Problem sets with solution guidance and examination specifications are under Assignments and Exams.

Part I · Risk, return and asset pricing Lectures 1–5

  1. Returns, Risk, and the Foundations of Asset Pricing
  2. Risk, Probability, and Expected Utility
  3. Mean-Variance Portfolio Theory and the Efficient Frontier
  4. CAPM, Beta, and Empirical Factor Models
  5. Arbitrage Pricing, Stochastic Discount Factors, and No-Arbitrage

Part II · Fixed income and derivatives Lectures 6–8

  1. Fixed-Income Securities, Bond Pricing, and Yield Curves
  2. Duration, Convexity, Immunization, and Term Structure
  3. Forwards, Futures, Options, and Swaps

Part III · Corporate finance and markets Lectures 9–11

  1. Corporate Finance: Capital Budgeting and Project Valuation
  2. Capital Structure, Leverage, and Payout Policy
  3. Market Efficiency, Information, Governance, and Event Studies

Part IV · Banking and risk Lectures 12–13

  1. Banking, Financial Intermediation, and Systemic Risk
  2. Financial Risk Management: VaR, Expected Shortfall, and Stress Testing

Part V · Behavioral and empirical finance Lectures 14–15

  1. Behavioral Finance: Beliefs, Preferences, and Market Outcomes
  2. Empirical Finance and Financial Econometrics

Assessment

Problem sets 25%; portfolio and risk assignment 20%; corporate finance case 15%; empirical finance project 20%; final examination 20%.

Core references

  • Bodie, Kane and Marcus, Investments.
  • Berk and DeMarzo, Corporate Finance.
  • Cochrane, Asset Pricing.
  • Hull, Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives.
  • Campbell, Lo and MacKinlay, The Econometrics of Financial Markets.
  • Tuckman and Serrat, Fixed Income Securities.
  • McNeil, Frey and Embrechts, Quantitative Risk Management.
