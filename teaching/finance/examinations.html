Midterm examination (course component within the 20% final-examination allocation only if instructor chooses to split it; otherwise use as formative assessment)

Duration: 120 minutes. A non-programmable calculator and one-page formula sheet are permitted.

Section A — Quantitative foundations (30%)

  • Simple and log return calculations.
  • Expected return, variance, covariance, and certainty equivalents.
  • Mean-variance portfolio calculations.

Section B — Asset pricing (35%)

  • CAPM beta and expected return.
  • Factor regression and alpha interpretation.
  • SDF pricing and no-arbitrage reasoning.

Section C — Fixed income (35%)

  • Coupon-bond pricing.
  • Spot and forward rates.
  • Duration and convexity approximations.

Final examination (20% course component)

Duration: 180 minutes. A non-programmable calculator and two-page formula sheet are permitted.

Section A — Portfolio and asset pricing (25%)

Derive and interpret a portfolio optimization result, CAPM relation, or factor-pricing restriction.

Section B — Fixed income and derivatives (25%)

Price a bond or derivative using supplied market inputs. Explain replication and risk-neutral valuation.

Section C — Corporate finance and institutions (25%)

Calculate project NPV, evaluate a financing choice, or analyze a bank balance-sheet shock.

Section D — Risk and empirical finance (25%)

Calculate and interpret VaR/ES, diagnose a financial econometric model, or design an event-study/forecast evaluation.

Grading rubric

  • Mathematical and financial derivation: 35%
  • Numerical accuracy and computational reasoning: 25%
  • Economic interpretation and assumptions: 25%
  • Clarity, units, and reproducibility: 15%

Empirical finance project

The project must include a research question, data dictionary, sample construction, return definitions, model specification, estimation details, diagnostics, out-of-sample or robustness analysis, and reproducible code. Any portfolio performance must be reported net of stated transaction-cost assumptions.
