# Finance
## Graduate and advanced undergraduate course in financial theory and markets

**Prerequisites:** Calculus, linear algebra, probability, statistics, and introductory economics.

## Learning outcomes
Students will value financial claims, quantify risk and return, derive portfolio choices, understand fixed-income instruments, analyze capital structure and investment, and apply empirical financial methods.

## 1. Financial markets and return measurement
Financial claims, primary and secondary markets, market participants, market microstructure, simple and log returns, holding-period returns, compounding, annualization, and real versus nominal returns. Explain arithmetic versus geometric average returns and the effects of volatility on compound growth.

## 2. Risk, probability and expected utility
Expected return E(R), variance Var(R), covariance, correlation, downside risk, and expected utility. Mean-variance preferences: U=E(R_p)-(γ/2)Var(R_p). Discuss risk aversion, certainty equivalents, and limitations of variance as a risk measure for asymmetric distributions.

## 3. Portfolio theory
For weights w, portfolio return R_p=w'R, expected return w'μ, and variance w'Σw. Derive the global minimum-variance portfolio under 1'w=1 using a Lagrangian. Derive the efficient frontier, tangency portfolio, risk-free borrowing/lending, short-sale constraints, and sensitivity to estimated means and covariances.

## 4. CAPM and multifactor models
Derive the CAPM relation E(R_i)-R_f=β_i[E(R_m)-R_f]. Define beta as covariance with market return divided by market variance. Discuss assumptions, security market line, Jensen's alpha, systematic versus idiosyncratic risk, and empirical tests. Introduce Fama-French factors, momentum, and factor-mimicking portfolios.

## 5. Arbitrage pricing and factor models
Law of one price, no-arbitrage, stochastic discount factors, linear factor pricing, and cross-sectional risk premia. Discuss factor identification, traded versus non-traded factors, estimation error, and model comparison.

## 6. Fixed-income securities
Bond cash flows, discounting, coupon and zero-coupon bonds, yield to maturity, spot and forward rates, clean and dirty prices, accrued interest, and credit spreads. Price a bond as the present value of promised cash flows under the appropriate discount curve.

## 7. Duration, convexity and term structure
Macaulay and modified duration, convexity, immunization, key-rate duration, yield curve construction, expectations hypothesis, term premium, and affine term-structure models. Derive first- and second-order price sensitivity to yield changes.

## 8. Derivatives
Forward and futures contracts, options, swaps, payoff diagrams, put-call parity, binomial option pricing, risk-neutral valuation, Black-Scholes assumptions, Greeks, hedging, and basis risk. Explain the difference between risk-neutral probabilities and physical probabilities.

## 9. Corporate finance and capital budgeting
Net present value, internal rate of return, payback limitations, weighted average cost of capital, project cash flows, taxes, depreciation, working capital, and sensitivity/scenario analysis. Establish NPV as the value-additive decision criterion under consistent discounting.

## 10. Capital structure and payout policy
Modigliani-Miller propositions, leverage, tax shields, distress costs, agency conflicts, pecking order, trade-off theory, dividends, repurchases, and signaling. State the assumptions under which benchmark irrelevance propositions hold.

## 11. Information, governance and market efficiency
Weak, semi-strong, and strong-form efficiency; event studies; abnormal returns; information asymmetry; adverse selection; moral hazard; corporate governance; and limits to arbitrage. Distinguish market efficiency from perfect forecasting.

## 12. Banking and financial institutions
Bank balance sheets, maturity transformation, liquidity risk, capital adequacy, leverage, credit risk, deposit insurance, runs, interbank markets, and systemic risk. Analyze duration mismatch and liquidity transformation.

## 13. Financial risk management
Value at Risk, expected shortfall, historical simulation, variance-covariance methods, Monte Carlo risk, stress testing, scenario analysis, credit value at risk, counterparty risk, and model risk. Explain tail dependence and limitations of normal approximations.

## 14. Behavioral finance
Prospect theory, loss aversion, reference dependence, overconfidence, limited attention, sentiment, disposition effect, and limits to arbitrage. Relate behavioral mechanisms to testable market outcomes.

## 15. Empirical finance and financial econometrics
Return predictability, event studies, panel regressions, ARCH/GARCH volatility, stochastic volatility, realized volatility, cointegration in prices and yields, market beta estimation, factor regressions, and forecast evaluation. Address look-ahead bias, survivorship bias, non-synchronous trading, data snooping, and robust standard errors.

## Worked problem set
1. Compute simple and log returns from a price sequence and explain their aggregation properties.
2. Derive global minimum-variance weights for two risky assets and interpret the covariance term.
3. Calculate CAPM expected return from a risk-free rate, beta, and market premium.
4. Price a coupon bond from its cash flows and spot curve; calculate duration and convexity.
5. Use put-call parity to infer the value of a call or put from the other option and underlying price.
6. Compute project NPV and explain why IRR can mis-rank mutually exclusive projects.
7. Compare VaR and expected shortfall for a distribution with a heavy left tail.

## Computational labs
Python: pandas, NumPy, SciPy, statsmodels, arch, and plotting for returns, portfolios, factor models, and volatility. R: PerformanceAnalytics, quantmod, rugarch, and sandwich. Spreadsheet exercises for bond pricing, duration, capital budgeting, and scenario analysis. Each lab includes data definitions, estimation choices, diagnostics, and reproducible scripts.

## Assessment
Problem sets 25%; portfolio and risk assignment 20%; corporate finance case 15%; empirical finance project 20%; final examination 20%.

## Core references
Bodie, Kane and Marcus, Investments; Berk and DeMarzo, Corporate Finance; Cochrane, Asset Pricing; Hull, Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives; Campbell, Lo and MacKinlay, The Econometrics of Financial Markets; Tuckman and Serrat, Fixed Income Securities.
