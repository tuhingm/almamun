01 / OVERVIEW

About this course

This is a 15-week training course in price action analysis of stocks. It is not a university teaching course: there are no exams and no grades. The aim is practical. By the end you will read a daily chart on your own, mark the key levels, recognise three signals (the pin bar, the inside bar and the fakey), count the confluence, set a stop loss and a target, size the position, manage the trade and record it in a trading journal.

What the course teaches

The method is price action: decisions come from the bars on the chart and the levels they respect. The course teaches one small set of tools and asks you to use them with discipline.

  • Reading the chart: the daily chart, the weekly chart, the trend and the swing points.
  • Key levels: support and resistance, and how a broken level turns into its opposite.
  • The 21 EMA: a moving average used as dynamic support and resistance in a trend.
  • Three signals: the pin bar, the inside bar and the fakey.
  • Confluence: trend, key level, 21 EMA and signal agreeing at the same place.
  • Money management: stop loss, risk reward, position sizing and the arithmetic of losing streaks.
  • Trade management: moving to breakeven, trailing the stop loss and taking the target.
  • Trading plan and psychology: a written plan, a trading journal and the habits of patience and discipline.

How each week works

Every week has the same shape: learning objectives, a lesson, a worked example on a chart, exercises with answers, and a chart lab. The lab is the most important part. You open your own daily charts of stocks and do the work yourself.

About the charts

All charts in this course use simulated prices that were built for teaching. They show the idea clearly, but they are not real stocks and do not show real results. In each chart lab you repeat the exercise on real stocks using your own charting platform and your broker's demo account.

Risk notice

This course is general education. It is not investment advice and it is not an offer to buy or sell any security. Trading stocks involves a risk of loss, and you can lose more than you expect. Do not trade money you cannot afford to lose. Past results, real or simulated, do not promise future results. Start on a demo account, and follow the rules of your own broker.
