# Econometrics — Examination Specifications

## Midterm examination

**Duration:** 120 minutes. **Total:** 100 marks. **Coverage:** Lectures 1–6.

### Section A — Concepts (20 marks)
Answer concise questions on conditional mean independence, projection matrices, Gauss–Markov assumptions, exact versus asymptotic inference, heteroskedasticity, and instrument validity.

### Section B — Derivations (40 marks)
1. Derive the OLS estimator and conditional variance (10).
2. Derive a joint Wald/F statistic for linear restrictions (10).
3. Derive omitted-variable bias and discuss its sign (10).
4. Derive the 2SLS estimator and state the identification conditions (10).

### Section C — Applied interpretation (20 marks)
Interpret a regression table containing OLS and IV estimates, classical and robust standard errors, and first-stage diagnostics. Identify which claims are supported and which require additional assumptions.

### Section D — Research design (20 marks)
Evaluate an instrument proposed for estimating the effect of education on wages. Discuss relevance, exclusion, weak identification, and the population represented by the IV estimand.

## Final examination

**Duration:** 180 minutes. **Total:** 100 marks. **Coverage:** Entire course.

### Section A — Theory and derivations (30 marks)
Choose and solve three problems on panel transformations, GMM moment conditions, stationarity, cointegration, VAR stability, or causal estimands.

### Section B — Model interpretation (25 marks)
Interpret an empirical output package covering a panel model and a time-series model. Discuss coefficients, standard errors, diagnostics, and identification.

### Section C — Applied research design (25 marks)
Design an empirical strategy for a policy question. Specify estimand, data structure, identifying variation, estimator, covariance estimator, diagnostics, and threats.

### Section D — Reproducibility (20 marks)
Evaluate a replication archive. Identify missing data documentation, code dependencies, seed control, transformation records, and reporting limitations.

## Examination standards
- Define symbols and dimensions.
- Show derivations; do not merely state formulas.
- Distinguish assumptions from testable implications.
- Interpret economic magnitudes and uncertainty.
- Do not claim causal identification from statistical significance alone.
- Credit is awarded for correct reasoning even when arithmetic errors occur downstream.
