Calculus · Course 03

Lecture Notes

Single- and multivariable calculus with rigorous foundations, optimization and dynamics for economics and statistics.

COURSE INFO

14 lecture sessions · Undergraduate, with graduate extensions

InstructorTuhin G. M. Al Mamun, Ph.D.
PrerequisitesAlgebra, functions, elementary trigonometry
SoftwarePython, R, MATLAB
Author's note

The design of this course, the lecture notes, the worked examples and the problem sets are my own. I prepared them from my training, research and teaching in economics. The theory and methods are standard, and the sources are listed in each lecture's readings.

— Tuhin G. M. Al Mamun, Ph.D.

Complete written notes for each session. Every set of notes opens with learning objectives and closes with a problem set, a computational lab and readings. Use Print / save as PDF on any lecture page for an offline copy.

Lec #Topic and objectivesLength
Part I · Single-variable analysis
1Limits, Continuity, and the Logic of Analysis
Use epsilon-delta definitions; evaluate limits; distinguish pointwise values from limiting behavior; establish continuity; apply the intermediate and extreme value theorems.
9 min read
2Sequences, Convergence, and Infinite Series
Define convergence rigorously; use monotone convergence and Cauchy criteria; test infinite series; distinguish absolute and conditional convergence; analyze power series.
8 min read
3Differentiation, Mean Value Theorems, and Comparative Rates
Derive derivatives from first principles; apply differentiation rules; use mean value theorems; interpret marginal effects, elasticities, and comparative rates.
8 min read
4Taylor Approximation, Convexity, and Single-Variable Optimization
Derive Taylor approximations; classify stationary points; use convexity; solve economic optimization problems and interpret first- and second-order conditions.
7 min read
5Integration, Riemann Sums, and the Fundamental Theorem
Define the Riemann integral; connect antiderivatives and accumulated area; apply substitution and integration by parts; evaluate improper integrals and interpret economic accumulation.
7 min read
Part II · Multivariable calculus
6Multivariable Functions, Limits, and Continuity
Analyze domains and level sets; define multivariate limits and continuity; distinguish pathwise behavior from genuine limits; interpret surfaces and economic production functions.
8 min read
7Partial Derivatives, Total Differentials, and the Chain Rule
Compute partial derivatives; interpret gradients and directional derivatives; derive total differentials; apply multivariate chain rules and Jacobians.
7 min read
8Hessians and Unconstrained Multivariable Optimization
Use Hessian matrices to classify curvature; derive multivariate first- and second-order conditions; apply optimization to utility, production, and likelihood functions.
8 min read
Part III · Optimization and implicit functions
9Equality Constraints and Lagrange Multipliers
Derive Lagrange conditions; interpret multipliers as shadow values; solve constrained optimization problems; understand regularity and second-order conditions.
8 min read
10Inequality Constraints and Karush–Kuhn–Tucker Conditions
State KKT conditions; identify active constraints; interpret complementary slackness; distinguish necessary and sufficient conditions; solve inequality-constrained economic problems.
8 min read
11Implicit and Inverse Function Theorems
State local implicit and inverse function results; derive comparative statics; analyze equilibrium systems using Jacobians; distinguish local from global uniqueness.
8 min read
Part IV · Multiple integration
12Multiple Integrals, Change of Variables, and Probability
Evaluate double and triple integrals; use Fubini's theorem; change coordinates with Jacobians; derive marginal densities and expectations.
7 min read
Part V · Dynamic systems
13Differential Equations and Continuous-Time Dynamics
Solve first-order ODEs; analyze second-order linear equations; study systems using eigenvalues; interpret steady states and stability in economic dynamics.
8 min read
14Difference Equations and Discrete-Time Dynamics
Solve first-order difference equations; analyze fixed points and local stability; study linear systems; apply discrete dynamics to economic adjustment and growth.
7 min read
