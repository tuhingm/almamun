# Calculus — Syllabus

**Course level:** Undergraduate mathematical foundations with graduate extensions for economics and statistics  
**Duration:** 14 weeks  
**Prerequisites:** Algebra, functions, and elementary trigonometry

## Course description
This course develops single- and multivariable calculus with rigorous foundations and applications to economics, statistics, and dynamic modeling. It emphasizes definitions, derivations, mathematical reasoning, optimization, and computational verification.

## Learning outcomes
By the end of the course, students should be able to:
1. Prove basic limit and continuity statements using epsilon-delta arguments.
2. Analyze sequences and series using standard convergence criteria.
3. Differentiate and integrate single-variable functions and interpret derivatives economically.
4. Use Taylor approximations, convexity, and optimization conditions.
5. Analyze multivariate functions using gradients, Jacobians, and Hessians.
6. Solve equality- and inequality-constrained optimization problems.
7. Apply implicit and inverse function theorems to comparative statics.
8. Evaluate multiple integrals and use Jacobians in probability transformations.
9. Analyze continuous- and discrete-time dynamic systems.
10. Verify analytical results using symbolic and numerical computation.

## Weekly schedule
| Week | Topic | Main output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Limits and continuity | Epsilon-delta proof |
| 2 | Sequences and series | Convergence analysis |
| 3 | Differentiation and mean value theorems | Derivative derivations |
| 4 | Taylor approximation, convexity, optimization | Single-variable optimization |
| 5 | Integration and fundamental theorem | Integration assignment |
| 6 | Multivariable limits and continuity | Surface and level-set analysis |
| 7 | Partial derivatives, differentials, chain rule | Gradient/Jacobian exercises |
| 8 | Hessians and unconstrained optimization | Midterm examination |
| 9 | Lagrange multipliers | Equality-constrained application |
| 10 | KKT conditions | Inequality-constrained application |
| 11 | Implicit and inverse function theorems | Comparative statics |
| 12 | Multiple integrals and Jacobians | Probability integration |
| 13 | Differential equations | Continuous-time dynamics |
| 14 | Difference equations | Discrete-time stability project |

## Assessment
- Problem sets and mathematical proofs: 30%
- Computational assignments: 15%
- Midterm examination: 20%
- Final examination: 25%
- Applied mathematical modeling project: 10%

## Expectations
Solutions must show mathematical reasoning, not only final answers. State domains, regularity assumptions, and constraint qualifications. Computational results must be reproducible and checked against analytical arguments where possible.

## Core references

- Spivak, *Calculus*.
- Simon and Blume, *Mathematics for Economists*.
- Chiang and Wainwright, *Fundamental Methods of Mathematical Economics*.
- Rudin, *Principles of Mathematical Analysis* (selected advanced topics).
