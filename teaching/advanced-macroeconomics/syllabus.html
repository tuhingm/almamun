Course description

This course develops dynamic macroeconomic theory from household and firm optimization to quantitative general-equilibrium models. It covers neoclassical growth, RBC models, nominal rigidities, monetary and fiscal policy, open-economy macroeconomics, incomplete markets, HANK models, and DSGE estimation.

Prerequisites

Intermediate macroeconomics; single- and multivariable calculus; linear algebra; probability; and introductory econometrics. Students should be able to manipulate constrained optimization problems and interpret matrix eigenvalues.

Learning outcomes

  1. Derive Euler equations, firm optimality conditions, and equilibrium restrictions.
  2. Solve steady-state and transition problems in dynamic macroeconomic systems.
  3. Analyze rational-expectations determinacy and policy transmission.
  4. Implement and diagnose quantitative DSGE models.
  5. Explain the role of heterogeneity and distributions in aggregate outcomes.
  6. Evaluate model fit, policy counterfactuals, and welfare.

Teaching and learning

Each week combines a formal lecture, derivation session, problem-solving seminar, and computational exercise. Computational work may use Python, Julia, MATLAB, or Dynare. Students must document software versions, parameter values, data transformations, and numerical diagnostics.

Assessment

  • Theory problem sets: 25%
  • DSGE replication project: 25%
  • Policy analysis: 15%
  • Midterm examination: 15%
  • Final research paper: 20%

Assessment standards

Mathematical derivations must state assumptions and timing conventions. Computational submissions must report convergence criteria, equilibrium residuals, and reproducibility information. Empirical or policy claims must be tied to model mechanisms and clearly distinguished from identifying assumptions.

Core texts

  • Ljungqvist and Sargent, Recursive Macroeconomic Theory.
  • Galí, Monetary Policy, Inflation, and the Business Cycle.
  • Woodford, Interest and Prices.
  • Heer and Maussner, Dynamic General Equilibrium Modeling.
  • Acemoglu, Introduction to Modern Economic Growth.
  • Obstfeld and Rogoff, Foundations of International Macroeconomics.
