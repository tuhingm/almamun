# Advanced Macroeconomics

<p class="lead">This course develops dynamic macroeconomic theory from household and firm optimization to quantitative general-equilibrium models. It covers neoclassical growth, RBC models, nominal rigidities, monetary and fiscal policy, open-economy macroeconomics, incomplete markets, HANK models, and DSGE estimation.</p>

## What you will learn

1. Derive Euler equations, firm optimality conditions, and equilibrium restrictions.
2. Solve steady-state and transition problems in dynamic macroeconomic systems.
3. Analyze rational-expectations determinacy and policy transmission.
4. Implement and diagnose quantitative DSGE models.
5. Explain the role of heterogeneity and distributions in aggregate outcomes.
6. Evaluate model fit, policy counterfactuals, and welfare.

## How the course is organized

Each lecture session has a full set of written notes: learning objectives, formal development with derivations, worked examples, a problem set, a computational lab and readings. Problem sets with solution guidance and examination specifications are under Assignments and Exams.

## Assessment

Theory problem sets 25%; DSGE replication project 25%; policy analysis 15%; midterm 15%; final research paper 20%.
