01 / OVERVIEW

About this course

This course develops dynamic macroeconomic theory from household and firm optimization to quantitative general-equilibrium models. It covers neoclassical growth, RBC models, nominal rigidities, monetary and fiscal policy, open-economy macroeconomics, incomplete markets, HANK models, and DSGE estimation.

What you will learn

  1. Derive Euler equations, firm optimality conditions, and equilibrium restrictions.
  2. Solve steady-state and transition problems in dynamic macroeconomic systems.
  3. Analyze rational-expectations determinacy and policy transmission.
  4. Implement and diagnose quantitative DSGE models.
  5. Explain the role of heterogeneity and distributions in aggregate outcomes.
  6. Evaluate model fit, policy counterfactuals, and welfare.

How the course is organized

Each lecture session has a full set of written notes: learning objectives, formal development with derivations, worked examples, a problem set, a computational lab and readings. Problem sets with solution guidance and examination specifications are under Assignments and Exams.

Part I · Neoclassical foundations Lectures 1–4

  1. The Dynamic Household: Intertemporal Choice and Euler Equations
  2. Firms, Capital Accumulation, and Investment
  3. Solow and Ramsey Growth: Steady States and Transitional Dynamics
  4. Real Business Cycle Models and Competitive Equilibrium

Part II · Nominal rigidities and policy Lectures 5–9

  1. Nominal Rigidities, Calvo Pricing, and the New Keynesian Phillips Curve
  2. The Three-Equation New Keynesian Model
  3. Monetary Policy: Rules, Commitment, and the Effective Lower Bound
  4. Fiscal Policy, Public Debt, and Monetary-Fiscal Interactions
  5. Open-Economy Macroeconomics and Exchange-Rate Dynamics

Part III · Solution and estimation Lectures 10–11

  1. DSGE Solution Methods: Linearization, Perturbation, and QZ
  2. DSGE Estimation: Likelihood, Bayesian Inference, and Model Evaluation

Part IV · Heterogeneous agents Lectures 12–13

  1. Heterogeneous-Agent Macroeconomics and Incomplete Markets
  2. HANK Models: Distribution Dynamics and Aggregate Transmission

Part V · Quantitative policy analysis Lecture 14

  1. Quantitative Macro Policy Analysis, Validation, and Replication

Assessment

Theory problem sets 25%; DSGE replication project 25%; policy analysis 15%; midterm 15%; final research paper 20%.

Core references

  • Ljungqvist and Sargent, Recursive Macroeconomic Theory.
  • Galí, Monetary Policy, Inflation, and the Business Cycle.
  • Woodford, Interest and Prices.
  • Heer and Maussner, Dynamic General Equilibrium Modeling.
  • Acemoglu, Introduction to Modern Economic Growth.
  • Obstfeld and Rogoff, Foundations of International Macroeconomics.
