LECTURE 09 / 15

Social Protection and Welfare Systems

Level: Graduate | Field: Public Policy and Human Development

Social insurance, social assistance, lifecycle risks, targeting, universalism, adequacy and fiscal sustainability.

Learning objectives

  1. Define the central concepts and distinguish related measures.
  2. Explain the analytical framework, assumptions and causal mechanisms.
  3. Apply the framework to a policy problem using appropriate evidence.
  4. Interpret distributional implications and communicate limitations.

1. Conceptual foundations

Social protection addresses lifecycle risks and shocks through social assistance, social insurance and labor-market measures. Assistance is generally non-contributory; insurance pools risks through contributions or public financing; labor-market interventions protect workers and support transitions. Programmes may be universal, categorical, means-tested or self-targeted. Each design has implications for coverage, adequacy, incentives, administrative cost and political sustainability.

2. Analytical framework

Targeting performance requires explicit denominators. Exclusion error is the share of eligible people who do not receive benefits; inclusion error is the share of recipients who are outside the intended target group. A programme can have low leakage but high exclusion when documentation or registration is difficult. Adequacy compares benefit value with a poverty line, consumption or prior earnings. Fragmented registries can create lifecycle gaps; integrated delivery and grievance systems can improve access and responsiveness.

3. Worked policy example

A transfer programme pays 50 currency units monthly to 10,000 enrolled households. Administrative records show 95% payment delivery, but a household survey finds that only 65% of eligible households are enrolled. Payment reliability conditional on enrolment is high, while coverage remains weak. Diagnose outreach, documentation, payment access and eligibility rules separately.

4. Seminar discussion

  1. Which outcome dimensions are hidden by the headline indicator?
  2. Which groups face different constraints or treatment?
  3. What evidence would distinguish competing explanations?
  4. What assumptions are required for the proposed analysis?

Problem set

  1. Construct a targeting confusion matrix and calculate inclusion and exclusion errors with stated denominators.
  2. Compare universal, categorical and means-tested transfers under an administrative-cost constraint.
  3. Assess benefit adequacy and indexation under inflation.

Applied policy lab

Map the social-protection system for one country or region across lifecycle risks. Identify programmes, financing, eligibility, benefit adequacy, coverage gaps and grievance mechanisms. Propose a reform and assess fiscal, administrative and distributional implications.

Required output: State the population, geography, period, indicator definitions, data sources and assumptions. Separate descriptive findings from causal claims.

Further reading

Barr, The Economics of the Welfare State; ILO, World Social Protection Report; Gentilini et al., research on social assistance and delivery systems.
