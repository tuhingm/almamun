LECTURE 08 / 15

Employment, Labor Markets and Decent Work

Level: Graduate | Field: Public Policy and Human Development

Labor supply and demand, informality, unemployment, job quality, wages and structural transformation.

Learning objectives

  1. Define the central concepts and distinguish related measures.
  2. Explain the analytical framework, assumptions and causal mechanisms.
  3. Apply the framework to a policy problem using appropriate evidence.
  4. Interpret distributional implications and communicate limitations.

1. Conceptual foundations

Labor-market diagnosis separates participation, employment, unemployment and inactivity. The unemployment rate is unemployed persons actively seeking and available for work divided by the labor force, not the working-age population. Informality, underemployment, unpaid family work, hours constraints and discouraged workers make headline unemployment incomplete. Decent work adds security, rights, safety, social protection, adequate earnings and worker voice to the employment count.

2. Analytical framework

In a competitive benchmark, labor demand follows the marginal product of labor and labor supply reflects the trade-off between work and non-work. Actual markets may feature monopsony, search frictions, segmentation, discrimination and institutional wage setting. The Mincer equation ln(w_i)=α+βS_i+γX_i+ε_i describes conditional wage differences but selection into employment and occupation complicates interpretation. Active labor-market policies include training, job-search assistance, wage subsidies and public employment services; assess additionality, displacement and deadweight costs.

3. Worked policy example

A training programme reports 500 completions and 300 placements. The placement rate is 60%, but this is not an impact estimate. If comparable nonparticipants also obtain jobs, additionality may be small. Track employment, earnings, hours, contract status and retention against a credible comparison group at multiple follow-ups.

4. Seminar discussion

  1. Which outcome dimensions are hidden by the headline indicator?
  2. Which groups face different constraints or treatment?
  3. What evidence would distinguish competing explanations?
  4. What assumptions are required for the proposed analysis?

Problem set

  1. Calculate unemployment, participation and employment-to-population rates from a labor-force table.
  2. Draw a labor-market diagram for a wage subsidy and discuss displacement.
  3. Specify an evaluation design for vocational training with outcomes at 6 and 18 months.

Applied policy lab

Prepare a labor-market diagnostic by age, gender, region and education. Include participation, unemployment, informality, earnings and job quality. Design a results framework for one active labor-market policy and specify the counterfactual needed to estimate additional jobs.

Required output: State the population, geography, period, indicator definitions, data sources and assumptions. Separate descriptive findings from causal claims.

Further reading

Borjas, Labor Economics; Fields, Working Hard, Working Poor; ILO, Decent Work Indicators.
