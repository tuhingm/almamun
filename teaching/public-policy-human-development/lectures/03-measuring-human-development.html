LECTURE 03 / 12

Measuring Human Development

HDI dimensions, life expectancy, education indicators, income measures, inequality-adjusted indicators and data comparability.

Learning objectives

  1. Define the key concepts and policy terminology used in this session.
  2. Explain the analytical framework and its assumptions.
  3. Apply the framework to a country or subnational policy problem.
  4. Interpret evidence and communicate its implications.

1. Conceptual framework

HDI dimensions, life expectancy, education indicators, income measures, inequality-adjusted indicators and data comparability. The analysis begins by defining the population, institutions, outcomes and time horizon. Distinguish the stated policy objective from the instrument selected to achieve it, and distinguish policy adoption from effective implementation.

2. Analytical development

Interpret a composite index and explain how normalization and aggregation affect comparisons. A useful policy assessment connects inputs and institutional capacity to implementation processes, outputs and final human-development outcomes. The framework should specify who benefits, who bears costs, how outcomes are measured and what contextual factors shape implementation.

Policy analysis principle.

Policy effectiveness depends not only on formal design but also on administrative capacity, incentives, coverage, take-up, service quality and the distribution of benefits and costs.

3. Worked policy application

Consider a hypothetical regional programme intended to improve a human-development outcome. Define the target population and baseline conditions; map the institutions responsible for delivery; identify the policy instrument and its eligibility rules; specify measurable intermediate and final outcomes; and assess whether administrative and fiscal resources are sufficient. Compare the proposed intervention with at least one alternative and state the evidence needed to judge performance.

4. Discussion questions

  • Which dimension of human development is the policy intended to improve?
  • Which groups may face barriers to access or take-up?
  • What implementation conditions are necessary?
  • Which indicators would capture both average outcomes and distributional change?

Problem set

  1. Define the principal concepts and explain their relevance to the policy question.
  2. Prepare a short evidence table with at least three indicators and their sources.
  3. Identify two implementation risks and propose mitigation strategies.

Applied policy activity

Select a country, region or population and prepare a one-page policy map: problem definition, affected groups, existing policy instruments, delivery institutions, outcome indicators and evidence gaps.

Further reading

Consult the relevant chapter and official data sources listed on the Readings page. Add one recent country-specific policy report or peer-reviewed study.
