LECTURE 14 / 15

Policy Evaluation, Evidence and Impact Assessment

Theory of change, logic models, monitoring indicators, randomized evaluations, quasi-experimental designs, process evaluation and evidence synthesis.

Learning objectives

  1. Define the key concepts and analytical questions for this session.
  2. Explain the underlying framework, assumptions and methodological choices.
  3. Apply the framework to a structured empirical or policy problem.
  4. Interpret findings and communicate implications and limitations.

1. Conceptual framework

Theory of change, logic models, monitoring indicators, randomized evaluations, quasi-experimental designs, process evaluation and evidence synthesis. Begin by defining the unit of analysis, target outcome and decision context. Distinguish the descriptive question from the explanatory or causal question, and make the assumptions behind the proposed analysis explicit.

2. Analytical development

Select an evaluation design appropriate to a policy question and distinguish implementation monitoring from impact evaluation. A rigorous application should document data sources, variable construction, analytical choices and uncertainty. Compare a baseline approach with a defensible alternative, and evaluate whether conclusions are robust to reasonable changes in assumptions or implementation.

Key analytical principle.

Interpret results relative to the target question, measurement choices, identifying assumptions and relevant comparison. Report limitations and uncertainty alongside substantive findings.

3. Worked application

Construct an applied case with a clearly defined research question, relevant population or dataset, measurable outcomes and a transparent analytical plan. Specify the baseline, conduct an alternative or sensitivity analysis, and prepare a concise interpretation that distinguishes evidence from assumptions.

4. Discussion questions

  • What is the principal target, estimand or policy objective?
  • Which assumptions are required for the analysis?
  • What sources of heterogeneity or measurement error matter?
  • Which robustness checks would materially strengthen the conclusion?

Problem set

  1. Explain the main framework and its assumptions.
  2. Work through a small numerical, computational or policy example.
  3. Identify two limitations and propose suitable sensitivity checks.

Applied activity

Prepare an applied policy assessment using a country or regional case. Specify the affected population, evidence base, implementation actors, distributional implications and measurable outcomes.

Further reading

Use the relevant core references and official data or software documentation listed on the Readings page, supplemented by recent peer-reviewed research.
