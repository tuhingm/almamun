LECTURE 14 / 15

Policy Evaluation, Evidence and Impact Assessment

Level: Graduate | Field: Public Policy and Human Development

Theory of change, estimands, randomized designs, quasi-experiments, cost-effectiveness and evidence synthesis.

Learning objectives

  1. Define the central concepts and distinguish related measures.
  2. Explain the analytical framework, assumptions and causal mechanisms.
  3. Apply the framework to a policy problem using appropriate evidence.
  4. Interpret distributional implications and communicate limitations.

1. Conceptual foundations

Monitoring describes implementation and outcomes; process evaluation studies delivery and mechanisms; impact evaluation estimates causal effects; economic evaluation compares costs and consequences. Define treatment, eligible population, assignment unit, outcome, follow-up horizon and estimand before selecting a method. The fundamental problem is that the same unit cannot simultaneously be observed under treatment and no treatment. A credible counterfactual must be constructed using a design and explicit assumptions.

2. Analytical framework

In a randomized trial, assignment makes treatment status independent of potential outcomes in expectation. The intention-to-treat effect is E[Y|Z=1]−E[Y|Z=0]. Difference-in-differences estimates (Y_T,post−Y_T,pre)−(Y_C,post−Y_C,pre), relying on parallel trends absent treatment. Regression discontinuity uses a treatment threshold and continuity near the cutoff. Instrumental variables require relevance, exclusion and monotonicity for a local treatment effect. Each design answers a specific estimand; standard errors must reflect the assignment and sampling structure.

3. Worked policy example

A district introduces a scholarship in 2025 while a comparison district does not. A difference-in-differences design compares pre-to-post changes. Plot several pre-policy periods, examine differential composition and concurrent programmes, test alternative comparison areas and report uncertainty clustered at the assignment level. A statistically significant estimate is not necessarily policy-relevant; report magnitude, confidence interval, baseline mean and cost per outcome gained.

4. Seminar discussion

  1. Define potential outcomes and an estimand for a scholarship. Calculate a difference-in-differences estimate from a four-cell table. Choose among RCT, DiD, RD and IV for four scenarios and state the identifying assumption. Explain the difference between intention-to-treat and treatment-on-the-treated effects?

Problem set

  1. Define the policy problem, target population and principal outcome.
  2. Specify the assumptions and evidence required to support the proposed analysis.
  3. Develop a distributional or sensitivity analysis and explain its implications.
  4. Identify two implementation risks and propose measurable mitigation strategies.

Applied policy lab

Write a pre-analysis evaluation protocol for a social programme. Include theory of change, estimand, design, sampling, power considerations, outcomes, data collection, threats to validity, attrition, costs and a communication plan for null or heterogeneous effects.

Required output: State the population, geography, period, indicator definitions, data sources and assumptions. Separate descriptive findings from causal claims.

Further reading

Gertler et al., Impact Evaluation in Practice; Imbens and Rubin, Causal Inference for Statistics, Social, and Biomedical Sciences; Angrist and Pischke, Mostly Harmless Econometrics.
