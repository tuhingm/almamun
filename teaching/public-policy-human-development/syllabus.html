COURSE FRAMEWORK

Syllabus

An interdisciplinary graduate course examining how public institutions and policy choices shape human capabilities and well-being. It covers human-development frameworks and measurement, poverty and inequality, education and health systems, employment, social protection, gender, governance and sustainable development, using comparative cases and applied policy exercises.

Learning outcomes

  1. Explain major approaches to human development, capabilities and well-being.
  2. Interpret human-development indicators and assess their measurement limits.
  3. Analyze poverty, inequality and social exclusion using multidimensional perspectives.
  4. Compare policy instruments in education, health, employment and social protection.
  5. Assess how institutions, governance and implementation shape policy outcomes.
  6. Develop an evidence-informed policy brief connecting a defined social problem to feasible policy options.

Prerequisites and resources

Introductory social science or policy knowledge; no advanced mathematics required. Resources: R, Python, spreadsheets and public data portals.

Teaching format

Lectures, guided examples, comparative policy cases, independent reading and applied assignments.

Assessment

Suggested weighting: participation 10%; problem sets 25%; applied policy assignments 25%; midterm 15%; final project 25%.
