# Statistics — Graduate Course Syllabus

## Description
A mathematical and applied course in probability, statistical inference, Bayesian methods, experimental design, regression, generalized linear models, nonparametric methods, power, mixed models, and multivariate analysis.

## Prerequisites
Calculus, linear algebra, and introductory statistics. Students should use R or Python for computational assignments.

## Learning outcomes
Students will formulate probability models; derive estimators and sampling distributions; conduct likelihood and Bayesian inference; design experiments; analyze factorial and hierarchical data; fit GLMs; plan power; and produce reproducible statistical analyses.

## 14-week plan

| Week | Lecture | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Probability models and expectation | Probability derivations |
| 2 | Distributions and multivariate models | Distribution and Jacobian exercises |
| 3 | Sampling and asymptotics | LLN/CLT and delta-method problems |
| 4 | Point estimation | MLE derivation |
| 5 | Confidence intervals and testing | Testing report |
| 6 | Bayesian inference | Conjugate posterior exercise |
| 7 | Experimental design | Randomization and blocking plan |
| 8 | ANOVA and multiple comparisons | Factorial analysis |
| 9 | Regression and GLMs | Logistic/count model lab |
| 10 | Nonparametric inference | Rank/permutation analysis |
| 11 | Power and sample size | Preregistered power plan |
| 12 | Mixed models | Hierarchical model analysis |
| 13 | Multivariate analysis | PCA report |
| 14 | Simulation and reproducibility | Statistical research project |

## Assessment

## Core references
Casella and Berger, *Statistical Inference*; Lehmann and Romano, *Testing Statistical Hypotheses*; van der Vaart, *Asymptotic Statistics*; Gelman et al., *Bayesian Data Analysis*; Montgomery, *Design and Analysis of Experiments*; McCullagh and Nelder, *Generalized Linear Models*.
