Probability, mathematical inference and experimental design.

Author's note

The design of this course, the lecture notes, the worked examples and the problem sets are my own. I prepared them from my training, research and teaching in economics. The theory and methods are standard, and the sources are listed in each lecture's readings.

Complete written notes for each session. Every set of notes opens with learning objectives and closes with a problem set, a computational lab and readings. Use Print / save as PDF on any lecture page for an offline copy.

Lec #Topic and objectivesLength
Part I · Probability and asymptotics
1Probability Models, Random Variables, and Expectation
Define probability spaces and random variables; derive expectations and moments; work with conditional distributions; distinguish independence from zero correlation; apply iterated expectations and the law of total variance.
9 min read
2Probability Distributions and Multivariate Models
Derive moments of standard distributions; transform random variables using Jacobians; obtain joint, marginal, and conditional distributions; interpret covariance matrices and multivariate normality.
8 min read
3Sampling Distributions and Asymptotic Theory
Distinguish sampling distributions from population distributions; apply LLN, CLT, Slutsky, continuous mapping, and delta methods; understand bootstrap approximation.
7 min read
Part II · Estimation and inference
4Point Estimation: Moments, Likelihood, and Optimality
Construct method-of-moments and maximum-likelihood estimators; derive score and information; distinguish unbiasedness, consistency, and efficiency; apply invariance and asymptotic theory.
7 min read
5Confidence Intervals and Hypothesis Testing
Construct confidence intervals; formulate tests; derive size and power; interpret p-values; understand likelihood-ratio, Wald, and score tests; distinguish statistical and practical significance.
8 min read
6Bayesian Inference, Conjugacy, and Posterior Computation
Derive posterior distributions; work with conjugate models; distinguish credible and confidence intervals; derive posterior predictions; assess prior sensitivity and MCMC computation.
9 min read
Part III · Experimental design and ANOVA
7Experimental Design, Randomization, and Blocking
Define experimental units, treatments, outcomes, and estimands; explain randomization, blocking, replication, factorial and split-plot designs; connect assignment mechanisms to design-based inference.
8 min read
8ANOVA, Linear Contrasts, and Multiple Comparisons
Derive one-way ANOVA; partition sums of squares; formulate contrasts; distinguish fixed and random effects; understand multiplicity adjustments and diagnostics.
8 min read
Part IV · Statistical modeling
9Linear Regression and Generalized Linear Models
Derive the linear model; interpret conditional mean parameters; specify GLMs; interpret logistic and Poisson regression; assess overdispersion and diagnostics.
8 min read
10Nonparametric Inference, Rank Tests, and Resampling
Use empirical distributions and quantiles; derive rank procedures; state the hypotheses actually tested; conduct permutation tests and bootstrap inference.
7 min read
11Statistical Power, Sample Size, and Equivalence
Define power and minimum detectable effects; calculate sample sizes; account for clustering and attrition; distinguish non-significance from evidence of equivalence.
8 min read
12Linear Mixed Models and Hierarchical Data
Specify random-intercept and random-slope models; distinguish within- and between-group effects; derive covariance structures and ICC; understand partial pooling and likelihood-based estimation.
9 min read
13Multivariate Analysis: PCA, Covariance, and Dimension Reduction
Analyze covariance matrices; derive principal components; interpret eigenvalues and loadings; distinguish PCA from factor analysis; understand scaling and validation.
8 min read
Part V · Statistical workflow
14Statistical Modeling Workflow, Simulation, and Reproducibility
Specify estimands and statistical models; design simulation studies; evaluate estimator performance; document computational decisions; report uncertainty and create reproducible archives.
8 min read
