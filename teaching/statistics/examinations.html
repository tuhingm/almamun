Midterm examination

Duration: 120 minutes. Total: 100 marks. Coverage: Lectures 1–6.

Section A — Probability and distributions (25)

Define probability spaces, derive moments, conditional expectation, covariance properties, and a transformation density.

Section B — Asymptotic theory (25)

Apply LLN/CLT, Slutsky, and delta method. Distinguish convergence in probability and distribution.

Section C — Estimation and inference (30)

Derive a likelihood estimator; construct a confidence interval; formulate a test and interpret its size, power, and p-value.

Section D — Bayesian inference (20)

Derive a conjugate posterior and compare posterior credible intervals with frequentist confidence intervals.

Final examination

Duration: 180 minutes. Total: 100 marks. Coverage: Entire course.

Section A — Experimental design and ANOVA (25)

Analyze a randomized-block or factorial design, specify the model, derive degrees of freedom, and define planned contrasts.

Section B — Regression and GLM (25)

Interpret logistic or Poisson regression output, calculate marginal effects, and assess model diagnostics.

Section C — Advanced methods (25)

Answer problems on nonparametric inference, power, mixed models, or PCA. State the inferential target and assumptions.

Section D — Applied statistical design (25)

Develop a statistical analysis plan with sampling design, estimand, model, multiplicity strategy, power justification, diagnostics, and reproducibility protocol.

Standards

  • Define symbols, parameters, and dimensions.
  • State assumptions before applying a theorem or test.
  • Distinguish model-based and design-based inference.
  • Interpret effect sizes and uncertainty, not only p-values.
  • Explain what diagnostics can and cannot establish.
  • Provide reproducible code and package versions for computational submissions.
