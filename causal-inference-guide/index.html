Causal Inference Research Guide

Choose an identification strategy from the assignment mechanism, treatment timing, comparison units, outcome structure and available variation. This guide covers conventional and staggered DiD, event studies, synthetic controls, generalized synthetic control, synthetic DiD, interrupted time series, structural-break interventions, RDD, IV and matching/weighting.

Identification workflow

Design before estimation

  1. Define the treatment, unit of assignment, exposure date and target population.
  2. Specify the estimand: ATE, ATT, group-time ATT, local effect, or unit-level treatment effect.
  3. Construct the counterfactual and state the identifying assumptions.
  4. Choose an estimator compatible with treatment timing and data structure.
  5. Diagnose pre-trends, overlap, donor fit, manipulation or instrument strength as relevant.
  6. Report uncertainty, dynamic effects, sensitivity and placebo analyses.

Method library

Identification strategies and estimators

Methods are grouped by their source of identifying variation. Related estimators are not interchangeable: they can target different treatment effects and rely on different assumptions.

01 · Difference-in-Differences

Conventional DiD

Two-group, two-period or multi-period design with a common treatment date. The canonical estimand is the ATT.

\(Y_{it}=\alpha_i+\lambda_t+\tau(D_i\times Post_t)+X'_{it}\beta+\varepsilon_{it}\)
  • Parallel trends absent treatment.
  • No anticipation and stable treatment composition.
  • Cluster inference at the treatment-assignment level.
02 · Staggered adoption

Staggered DiD

Units adopt treatment in different periods. Estimate group-time effects rather than relying mechanically on a conventional TWFE coefficient.

\(ATT(g,t)=E[Y_t(1)-Y_t(0)\mid G=g]\)
  • Callaway–Sant’Anna group-time ATT.
  • Sun–Abraham interaction-weighted event study.
  • Imputation and two-stage DiD estimators.
03 · Dynamic effects

Event-study DiD

Trace effects relative to treatment time and inspect pre-treatment coefficients. In staggered designs use an estimator robust to heterogeneous treatment effects.

\(Y_{it}=\alpha_i+\lambda_t+\sum_{k\ne -1}\beta_k 1[t-G_i=k]+\varepsilon_{it}\)
  • Assess pre-treatment coefficients jointly and substantively.
  • Use appropriate cohort-time aggregation.
  • Do not treat insignificant leads as proof of parallel trends.
04 · Donor-pool counterfactual

Synthetic Control (SCM)

Construct a weighted combination of untreated donor units to approximate the treated unit's pre-intervention trajectory.

\(\hat{Y}_{1t}(0)=\sum_{j=2}^{J+1}w_jY_{jt},\quad w_j\ge0,\;\sum_jw_j=1\)
  • Strong pre-treatment fit and credible donor pool.
  • Placebo-in-space and placebo-in-time analyses.
  • Check donor contamination and extrapolation.
05 · Interactive fixed effects

Generalized Synthetic Control (GSC)

Xu's generalized SCM uses an interactive fixed-effects structure to estimate latent common factors and construct untreated potential outcomes.

\(Y_{it}(0)=X'_{it}\beta+\lambda_i' f_t+\varepsilon_{it}\)
  • Useful with multiple treated units and latent common factors.
  • Requires suitable untreated observations to estimate factors.
  • Assess factor rank and pre-treatment fit.
06 · Weighted DiD and SCM

Synthetic DiD (SDID)

Combines unit and time weighting with a DiD comparison. Useful when treated and control units differ in pre-treatment outcome paths.

\(\hat\tau_{SDID}=\text{weighted DiD contrast}\)
  • Estimate unit and time weights from pre-treatment data.
  • Inspect weight concentration and pre-fit.
  • Use inference appropriate to the number of treated units and panel structure.
07 · Single intervention series

Interrupted Time Series (ITS)

Estimate immediate level and slope changes after a known intervention in a sufficiently long time series.

\(Y_t=\beta_0+\beta_1t+\beta_2D_t+\beta_3(t-T_0)D_t+u_t\)
  • Model seasonality, serial correlation and concurrent interventions.
  • Check alternative intervention dates and trend forms.
  • A comparison series strengthens the design when available.
08 · Structural break

Intervention dummies and break models

Pulse, step and slope-change dummies represent a hypothesized break. Break tests can locate unknown changes; causal interpretation requires an intervention-based counterfactual.

\(Y_t=\alpha+\beta t+\delta_1D_t+\delta_2(t-T_0)D_t+u_t\)
  • Known break: intervention dummy / segmented regression.
  • Unknown break: Bai–Perron or single-break tests as diagnostics.
  • Distinguish a statistical break from a causal policy effect.
09 · Threshold assignment

Regression Discontinuity (RDD)

Exploit discontinuous treatment assignment at a known cutoff in a running variable.

\(\tau_{RD}=\lim_{r\downarrow c}E[Y\mid R=r]-\lim_{r\uparrow c}E[Y\mid R=r]\)
  • Continuity of potential outcomes at the cutoff.
  • Check sorting/manipulation and covariate continuity.
  • Report bandwidth and polynomial sensitivity; local linear is a standard baseline.
10 · Exogenous instrument

Instrumental Variables (IV / 2SLS)

Use an instrument that shifts endogenous treatment but affects the outcome only through treatment, under the specified design.

\(D_i=Z_i'\pi+X_i'\gamma+v_i,\quad Y_i=\tau\hat D_i+X_i'\beta+u_i\)
  • Relevance, exclusion and independence.
  • Weak-instrument-robust inference where needed.
  • For binary treatment, interpret LATE under monotonicity.
11 · Selection adjustment

Matching, IPW and doubly robust methods

Adjust for selection on observed covariates using propensity scores, inverse-probability weights or outcome/treatment models.

\(ATT=E[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid D=1]\)
  • Conditional exchangeability and overlap.
  • Assess balance after weighting/matching.
  • Doubly robust estimators combine treatment and outcome models.
Implementation patterns

Python and Stata starting points

These are compact templates. Replace variable names and verify package syntax/version against your installed release before estimation.

Python · DiD with unit and time effects

Two-way fixed-effects baseline for a conventional common-timing design. Use a staggered-adoption estimator instead when treatment timing or effects vary across cohorts.

Show Python template
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf

# df: unit, year, outcome, treated, post
df["did"] = df["treated"] * df["post"]
fit = smf.ols(
    "outcome ~ did + C(unit) + C(year)",
    data=df
).fit(
    cov_type="cluster",
    cov_kwds={"groups": df["unit"]}
)
print(fit.summary())

Stata · conventional and staggered DiD

Stata's built-in commands support standard and heterogeneous DiD workflows. For staggered timing, report group-time effects and aggregation choices.

Show Stata template
* Conventional DiD
xtset unit year
gen did = treated * post
xtreg outcome did i.year, fe vce(cluster unit)

* Staggered adoption (built-in; treatment timing gvar)
* gvar = first treatment year; 0 for never treated
xthdidregress aipw (outcome covariates) ///
    (treatment), group(unit) time(year)

Stata · Callaway–Sant'Anna

Community-contributed implementation for group-time ATT. Confirm the control-group definition and aggregation used in the paper.

Show Stata template
ssc install csdid, replace
xtset unit year
csdid outcome covariates, ivar(unit) time(year) ///
    gvar(first_treat_year) notyet method(dripw)
estat event
estat simple
estat group

Stata · structural break / ITS

Segmented regression with level and slope changes. Add seasonal terms and model serial correlation as required by the data frequency.

Show Stata template
tsset year
gen time = year - year[1]
gen post = year >= intervention_year
gen post_time = (year - intervention_year) * post
newey outcome time post post_time, lag(4)

Stata · RDD and IV

Specialized commands provide bandwidth selection and robust bias-corrected RDD inference; standard 2SLS is shown separately.

Show Stata template
ssc install rdrobust, replace
rdrobust outcome running_variable, c(0)

* 2SLS
ivregress 2sls outcome controls ///
    (treatment = instrument), vce(robust)
estat firststage
estat endogenous

R / Stata ecosystem · SCM, GSC and SDID

Use estimators designed for synthetic counterfactuals rather than treating a generic fixed-effects regression as a substitute.

  • SCM: R Synth; Stata synth.
  • GSC: R gsynth (Xu).
  • SDID: R synthdid; Stata implementations are available as community commands.
  • Staggered event studies: R did, fixest; Stata eventstudyinteract or csdid.
Interpretation rule: A statistically significant post dummy or detected structural break is not, by itself, a causal estimate. The causal claim depends on the counterfactual design, treatment assignment, timing, assumptions and inference procedure.
Recommended workflow

Minimum reporting checklist

  • Define treatment, control, timing, exposure, unit of assignment and target estimand.
  • Write the potential-outcomes or structural identifying assumptions explicitly.
  • Explain the comparison group/donor pool and treatment contamination risk.
  • Show pre-treatment fit or trends and report diagnostics appropriate to the design.
  • Use inference at the treatment-assignment level; address few clusters where relevant.
  • Report dynamic and subgroup effects without data-driven specification search.
  • Include placebo, sensitivity, alternative comparison groups and specification checks.
  • State limitations on external validity and the population to which the estimate applies.
