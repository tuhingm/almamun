Friedman test

The Friedman test is the rank-based test for a difference among k related samples (a randomised complete block design), and Kendall's W measures the agreement between blocks.

Overview

The Friedman test is the rank-based test for a difference among k related samples (a randomised complete block design), and Kendall's W measures the agreement between blocks.

Validated Compared with reference software on identical data and specifications, and checked by Monte Carlo simulation.

Run it on your dataTechnical validation report

Mathematical formulation

Within each block rank the k observations; R_j = rank sum of condition j over n blocks.
χ²_F = [12 / (n k (k + 1))] Σ R_j² − 3n(k + 1), tie-corrected by 1 − Σ(t³ − t)/(n k (k² − 1)); χ²_F ≈ χ²(k − 1).
Kendall's W = χ²_F / (n(k − 1)).
Pairwise: Nemenyi (studentised range) and Conover (t on rank sums) with Holm adjustment.

Assumptions

  • Blocks (subjects) independent of each other.
  • Observations within a block are ordinal or continuous and can be ranked.
  • Complete blocks.

Diagnostics and interpretation

  • W near 0 means no agreement among blocks about the ordering of conditions, W near 1 perfect agreement.

The diagnostic tables are part of the results above (assumption checks, fit statistics, residual or goodness-of-fit tables).

Limitations

  • The χ² approximation is poor for very few blocks.
  • The test uses only within-block information, so it is less powerful than a rank-ANOVA on aligned ranks in some designs.
