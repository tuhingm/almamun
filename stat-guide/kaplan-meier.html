Kaplan-Meier estimator and log-rank test

The Kaplan-Meier estimator gives a nonparametric estimate of the survival function from right-censored data, and weighted log-rank tests compare survival curves between groups.

Overview

The Kaplan-Meier estimator gives a nonparametric estimate of the survival function from right-censored data, and weighted log-rank tests compare survival curves between groups.

Validated Compared with reference software on identical data and specifications, and checked by Monte Carlo simulation.

Run it on your dataTechnical validation report

Mathematical formulation

Ŝ(t) = Π_{t_i ≤ t} (1 − d_i/n_i), d_i events and n_i at risk at time t_i.
Greenwood variance: Var Ŝ(t) = Ŝ(t)² Σ d_i/[n_i(n_i − d_i)].
Confidence intervals by the log-log (default), log or plain transformation.
Weighted log-rank: Z = Σ w_i (d_1i − E_1i) / sqrt Σ w_i² V_i with w_i = 1 (log-rank), n_i (Gehan-Breslow) or Peto-Peto S̃(t_i); χ² with k − 1 df for k groups.
Restricted mean survival time = ∫_0^τ Ŝ(t) dt with standard error from the Greenwood-type variance.

Assumptions

  • Censoring is independent of the event time (non-informative).
  • Subjects are independent.
  • For the log-rank test, proportional hazards makes it most powerful; the Gehan and Peto-Peto weights are less sensitive to late differences.

Estimation algorithm

  • Sorting and risk-set counting in a single pass; vectorised group-wise estimators; hypergeometric variance in the log-rank test.

Diagnostics and interpretation

  • Read the curve and its confidence band together; the median and the restricted mean summarise it; the log-rank p-value tests equality of the whole curves.

The diagnostic tables are part of the results above (assumption checks, fit statistics, residual or goodness-of-fit tables).

Limitations

  • Median survival is undefined when the curve does not fall to 0.5.
  • Tail estimates with few at risk are unstable.
