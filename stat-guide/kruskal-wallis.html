Kruskal-Wallis test

The Kruskal-Wallis test compares three or more independent groups through the ranks of all observations; it is the rank analogue of one-way ANOVA.

Overview

The Kruskal-Wallis test compares three or more independent groups through the ranks of all observations; it is the rank analogue of one-way ANOVA.

Validated Compared with reference software on identical data and specifications, and checked by Monte Carlo simulation.

Run it on your dataTechnical validation report

Mathematical formulation

H = [12 / (N(N + 1))] Σ R_i²/n_i − 3(N + 1), R_i = rank sum of group i.
Tie correction: H_c = H / [1 − Σ(t³ − t)/(N³ − N)].
Under H0, H_c is approximately χ²(k − 1).
Effect sizes: ε² = H(N + 1)/(N² − 1) and η²_H = (H − k + 1)/(N − k). Dunn: z_ij = (R̄_i − R̄_j)/sqrt{[N(N + 1)/12 − Σ(t³ − t)/(12(N − 1))](1/n_i + 1/n_j)} with a multiplicity adjustment.

Assumptions

  • Independent observations within and between groups.
  • Ordinal or continuous outcome.
  • Same distribution shape in all groups for a location interpretation.

Diagnostics and interpretation

  • A significant H says at least one group tends to give larger or smaller values than another.
  • Use the Dunn table for pairs; its adjusted p-values control the family-wise error rate (Holm, Bonferroni) or the false discovery rate (Benjamini-Hochberg).

The diagnostic tables are part of the results above (assumption checks, fit statistics, residual or goodness-of-fit tables).

Limitations

  • The χ² approximation is poor when groups are very small.
  • The omnibus test does not locate differences: use the Dunn table.
