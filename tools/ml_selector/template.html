How the tool decides. Prediction. (A1) A date column with at least #MINTS# periods marks a time series: each series is tested with ADF and KPSS, I(1) series are differenced, and validation uses rolling-origin splits that never shuffle time. (A2) With fewer than #SMALLN# observations only regularised linear models are used. (A3) Otherwise a random forest is compared with LASSO by five-fold cross-validated RMSE; if the forest is at least #GAIN#% better, the data are treated as nonlinear. (A4) Linear: if any two predictors are correlated above #HIGHR#, the elastic net; otherwise LASSO if it keeps fewer than half of the predictors at the one-standard-error penalty, and ridge if it keeps more. (A5) Nonlinear: a random forest below #BIGN# observations, XGBoost (gradient boosting) above. Causal effect. (B1) If confounders are unobserved and there is no instrument, machine learning cannot help. (B2) With fewer than #FEWCTRL# controls that enter linearly (the forest does not beat LASSO by #GAIN#% for the outcome or the treatment), OLS is enough. (B3) An instrument leads to DML-IV (the first-stage F should be at least #WEAKF#); a binary treatment to the interactive model, after checking that the propensity scores lie in [#OVLO#, #OVHI#]; a continuous treatment to the partially linear model. (B4) DML is estimated with LASSO and random-forest learners, five-fold cross-fitting; if the two differ by more than one standard error, the forest is reported. (B5) With at least #CFN# observations and a question about who is affected most, a causal forest. These cut-offs are common rules of thumb, like T < 15 in the Panel Data Guide; report the choice and its robustness.
